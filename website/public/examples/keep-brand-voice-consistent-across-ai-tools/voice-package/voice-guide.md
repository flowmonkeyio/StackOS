# Voice guide for the fictional Draft Pier example

Revision: 1.0
Owner: example editorial lead
Status: approved for this fictional exercise only

## Stable choices

Write to a small team member who has a draft to review. Name the action and
explain what happens next. Use ordinary words, direct verbs and specific limits.
Avoid claims such as effortless, seamless, revolutionary, guaranteed or game-changing.
Do not promise a time saving or a business result without a factual source.

## Tone for an in-app feature announcement

Be warm and briefly playful. Open with one short harbor or navigation metaphor,
then explain the actual behavior in plain words. Use at most one metaphor.
The metaphor must not replace the practical explanation.

## Approved expression example

“Give your draft a place to land. Assign a reviewer, then leave a note about
the decision you need.”

This example endorses a brief metaphor followed by a concrete action. It does
not establish that Draft Pier has reviewer assignment or comments. Product
facts must come from the source material supplied for the current task.

## Weak expression example

“Unlock effortless collaboration with our revolutionary review experience.”

This is weak because it substitutes praise for behavior. It tells a reader
neither what to do nor what the product does. This is an example written for
the exercise, not a quotation from a customer or a published brand.

## Boundary

The guide owns expression. The task's facts file owns behavior, timing and
limits. A support reply may need a calmer tone than this announcement. Do not
transfer the announcement's playful opening to an error message automatically.
