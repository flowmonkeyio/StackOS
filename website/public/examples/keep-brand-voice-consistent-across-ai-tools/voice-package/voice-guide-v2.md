# Voice guide for the fictional Draft Pier example

Revision: 2.0
Owner: example editorial lead
Status: approved for this fictional exercise only; replaces revision 1.0

## Stable choices

Write to a small team member who has a draft to review. Name the action and
explain what happens next. Use ordinary words, direct verbs and specific limits.
Avoid claims such as effortless, seamless, revolutionary, guaranteed or game-changing.
Do not promise a time saving or a business result without a factual source.

## Revised tone for an in-app feature announcement

Be warm and literal. Remove harbor and navigation metaphors from announcements.
Open with the concrete action a writer can take. The first sentence must name
the review reminder or the assigned reviewer. Do not begin with a slogan.

## Approved expression example

“Turn on a review reminder for the colleague assigned to your draft. Include
the decision you need in your note.”

This example endorses a direct action and a named recipient. It does not
establish a comments feature or any reminder schedule. Product facts must
come from the source material supplied for the current task.

## Weak expression example

“Give your draft a place to land.”

This formerly approved opening is now too indirect for this announcement
surface. The editorial decision changed; the sentence did not become
factually false. Replace it when it appears in an affected new announcement.

## Boundary

The guide owns expression. The task's facts file owns behavior, timing and
limits. This revision changes announcement tone, not the product behavior.
It does not retroactively establish that all earlier writing was unusable.
