# Natural writing and evidence for voice

Reference revision: `1.0.0`. Research checked: 2026-09-29 UTC.

Use this reference within the existing evidence, writing, and review work. The
project's approved foundation, representative examples, current request, and later
operator corrections determine its voice. These are general editorial methods;
they prescribe no personal identity, channel, tone, or narrative formula.
Consult relevant research when a decision needs it, without repeating a literature
review for every draft. No additional stage, role, score, or runtime check is required.

## Preserve useful source context

Give the author and reviewers the source material that affects the piece: original
wording, specific examples, relevant surrounding context, and known limits. A compact
brief helps navigation, but should not replace the detail needed to explain a choice
or compare expression. Keep source, date, version, and public-use status traceable.

Distinguish what happened, what the operator believes, what research reports, and
what the writer infers. A workflow describes intended behavior; it does not prove
an incident or successful outcome. A quoted opinion establishes that view, not its
prevalence. Keep counterevidence when it changes the meaning or conclusion.

Use evidence proportionate to the claim. An attributable firsthand account can
support ordinary personal experience; it does not establish measured improvement
or general causation. Reuse available material and ask only for a consequential gap.

## Keep endorsement and expression distinct

Record the basis and limits of each useful comparison in the existing evidence
context, without inventing a new evidence schema:

- An approved position supports what the person means, not a particular cadence.
- Observed wording is evidence of expression in its original situation.
- An endorsed example supports only the scope actually endorsed; a favorable
  response to a complete piece does not separately approve every sentence.
- A reviewer pass is a review result, not proof of operator endorsement.
- Later corrections remain relevant even when an earlier draft passed review.
- New calibration rewrites are proposals until endorsed. Repetition or silence
  does not promote them into approved examples.

Compare directness, reader relationship, detail, reasoning, and rhythm. Do not copy
an example's opening phrase, paragraph sequence, or ending as a permanent template.
If a missing example materially limits the judgment, name that specific gap.

## Establish the point before polishing

Privately settle what the reader should understand, why it matters, and which
evidence carries the explanation. Let the public form follow the material. An
opening should make a supported promise that the body fulfills. When alternatives
would help, compare genuinely different supported approaches; changing synonyms
does not create a different angle. No fixed number of hooks is required.

For experience-led work, make two separate judgments:

- **Experience grounding:** is the problem, action, or judgment attributable to
  the speaker? Separate actual experience from an explicitly hypothetical example.
- **Solution usefulness:** can the reader understand what changed or was tried,
  why it might address the problem, and what remains uncertain?

A true anecdote can lack useful explanation; a detailed procedure can lack personal
experience. A partial fix or unsuccessful attempt can still be useful. Do not invent
a success story, feelings, dialogue, vulnerability, or a transformation to fill gaps.
Analysis, instructions, and source commentary need no personal incident unless the
selected angle claims one.

## Read the whole piece first

Assess the actual point, reasoning, and positive voice comparison before replacing
individual words. Inspect repeated announcements, evenly developed consequences,
manufactured contrasts, padded transitions, and conclusions that merely restate the
opening. Ask what effect they have in this piece, not whether a pattern exists.

Clear exposition, early answers, lists, qualifications, questions, and summaries can
all serve the reader. Do not force their absence or randomize structure to appear
distinctive. Preserve necessary examples, causal relationships, and uncertainty.
An ordinary explanation may be the right form; unusual form is not proof of quality.

Prefer familiar precise words and make actors and actions clear when they matter.
Explain necessary terms without stripping useful technical detail. Break paragraphs
when the thought changes. Ordinary punctuation, passive voice, contractions, and
varied sentence lengths remain available. No isolated word or symbol proves a defect.

Choose tone for the material and approved project voice. Warmth does not require
private disclosure; humor does not require a joke quota. Keep important factual
meaning understandable. Avoid forced fragments, misspellings, slang, or roughness.
End when the reader has the necessary thought; a recap, question, or invitation is
optional unless the actual destination contract requires it.

## Review and repair within existing roles

The author compares the exact draft with the selected examples before handoff. The
independent voice reviewer examines complete prose and gives concrete comparisons,
including the limits of the available evidence. Claim and disclosure decisions keep
their existing owners. Coherence and watchlist compliance alone do not prove voice fit.

Tie each necessary repair to a passage or justified omission, its reader effect,
the evidence or request, and the smallest useful change. Separate preference from
a defect. A weak point returns to direction, missing support to evidence, and local
wording to the author. Repair composition before cosmetic substitutions when the
whole passage is generic. Recheck affected text and dimensions after changes.

## Research basis and limits

These sources inform editorial questions. The working choices above also require
project evidence and operator judgment; the papers do not validate every sentence
of this reference. None establishes an ideal voice or a promised engagement lift.

- **Grammar and register:** Reinhart et al., [Do LLMs write like humans?](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2422455122)
  (PNAS, February 2025), compares parallel English corpora across genres using
  GPT-4o and Llama 3 variants. Differences in grammar and rhetoric concern the
  sampled models, continuation prompts, and registers; they justify contextual
  revision, not a universal ban on nominalizations or passive voice.
- **Vocabulary:** Kobak et al., [excess vocabulary in biomedical publications](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07016)
  (Science Advances, July 2025; linked author preprint), analyzes over 15 million
  PubMed abstracts from 2010–2024. Aggregate word-frequency shifts do not identify
  an individual author's process or make the same vocabulary unsuitable elsewhere.
- **Structure:** Madler, [SlopShape v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.15369v3)
  (preprint, September 28, 2026), compares 2,250 historical commercial posts with
  11,250 generated counterparts. Brief loss, single-pass generation, historical
  comparison, restricted rewording, and a software/US-heavy corpus constrain the
  result. Human validation covered 12 posts and 20 features; the author discloses
  a commercial interest. It measures detection, not reader preference or engagement.
  This revision removes format-sensitive features; do not mix its results with
  [v2, September 17](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15369v2). Results were not reproduced here.
- **Detection limits:** Dugan et al., [RAID](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.674/)
  (ACL, August 2024), evaluates twelve detectors across models, domains, and
  generation variations. Their vulnerabilities support caution about the evaluated
  detectors, not a claim that every detector always fails. Detector scores do not
  measure fidelity to a particular voice and are excluded from editorial approval.
- **Clarity:** Shulman, Markowitz, and Rogers, [Reading dies in complexity](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adn2555)
  (Science Advances, June 2024), analyzes over 30,000 news headline experiments.
  Headline variants were randomized; simplicity was analyzed as a feature. The
  findings support considering familiar language, not a universal post length,
  long-term trust claim, or preference attributed to an entire generation.
- **Tone:** Fiske, Cuddy, and Glick, [warmth and competence](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17188552/)
  (review, February 2007), concerns interpersonal and group impressions. Applying
  it to respectful explanation is an editorial inference; it establishes no tone
  ratio, required sequence, or content-performance multiplier.
- **Practical clarity guidance:** GOV.UK, [Use clear language](https://guidance.publishing.service.gov.uk/writing-to-gov-uk-standards/writing-guidelines/clear-language/)
  (accessed September 2026), offers service-writing guidance, not an engagement
  experiment. Adopt useful clarity principles without importing institutional voice.

Do not optimize prose to evade detection or import detector instruments, reserved
prompts, feature quotas, rarity targets, or universal virality formulas. Retain the
source version and limits when adapting a finding. Evaluate actual reader outcomes
separately; organic differences in topic, audience, timing, and distribution do not
by themselves establish that a tone or wording change caused the result.
